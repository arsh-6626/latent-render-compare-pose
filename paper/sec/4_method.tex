\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\method has two parts. A world model $\WM$ renders the DINOv2 tokens of an object at an arbitrary pose (\cref{sec:obs,sec:wm}). It is
a feature field, fitted first and then frozen (\cref{sec:wmtrain}). A diffusion denoiser reads the field's rendering at its current
hypothesis at every step (\cref{sec:den}), and the same rendering ranks complete samples (\cref{sec:select}). \Cref{fig:method} gives
an overview.

\subsection{Observation tokens}
\label{sec:obs}
The input is a $224\times224$ crop $I$ of the object, cut and resized as in LiePose (dynamic zoom-in at training time). Pixels outside
the visible mask are set to zero when a mask is part of the protocol (\cref{sec:protocol}). A frozen DINOv2
ViT-S/14~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} maps the crop to $16\times16$ patch tokens of dimension 384, which we average-pool $2\times2$ into
$\zobs\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times384}$. A per-token weight $w_p=w_0+m_p$ emphasises the object: $m_p\in\{0,1\}$ is the max-pooled visible mask
dilated by one token, and $w_0=0.05$ keeps a small weight on background tokens. Where no mask is available (SPEED+), $m_p=1$. The crop
is described by its \emph{crop camera} $k$: the zoom, the crop origin, the box size and the image's intrinsics. $k$ contains no pose
information, and it maps a crop-relative pose (\cref{sec:background}) to the camera frame. Tokens of all training crops are computed
once and cached.

\subsection{A feature-field world model}
\label{sec:wm}
The world model must say what the tokens of the object would be at any pose, in the observation's crop. We represent the object by a
field over a box of half-size $r_o$ around its origin. Tri-plane features~\cite{chan2022eg3d} (three $128\times128$ planes with 32
channels, bilinearly sampled and summed) and Fourier features of the point position enter a two-layer MLP of width 128, which outputs a
density $\sigma(x)\ge0$ and a latent $\hlat(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$; outside the box the density is zero. On T-LESS each object has its own
planes, and the MLP and the heads below are shared by all objects.

\paragraph{Rendering.}
Given a pose hypothesis $g$ and the crop camera $k$, we cast one ray through each of $16\times16$ sub-patch centres of the crop and
place 32 stratified samples per ray within $\pm r_o$ of the object centre's depth. Standard volume rendering~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf}
gives per ray the weights of the samples, the opacity $\opa$ (the sum of the weights) and the expected latent of the surface the ray hits,
$\bar\hlat=\sum_j w_j\hlat(x_j)/\opa$. A head maps $\bar\hlat$ and the view direction in the object frame to a 384-dimensional token
feature; rays that miss the object see a background feature. Both are modulated by an appearance code $a$ computed from the
\emph{observation's own} token statistics (per-channel mean and standard deviation over its 64 tokens), in the spirit of per-image
appearance codes~\cite{martinbrualla2021nerfw}. The code absorbs global lighting and background, and it is the same for every pose
hypothesis of an observation, so it cannot by itself rank poses. Feature, opacity and latent are average-pooled $2\times2$ to the
$8\times8$ token grid:
\begin{equation}
\big(\zhat(g),\ \opa(g),\ \hlat(g)\big) = \WM(g,\ k,\ a)\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times384}\times\mathbb{R}^{64}\times\mathbb{R}^{64\times128}.
\label{eq:wm}
\end{equation}
The rendering is exact in the pose: a rotation of the hypothesis by $1^\circ$ moves every ray's samples by $1^\circ$ in the object frame.
Nothing in $\zhat$ comes from another image.

\paragraph{Box size.}
The field needs only a bound on the object's extent, no surface. On SPEED+ the box is fixed at $r_o=1$\,m around the spacecraft origin.
On T-LESS, $r_o$ is $1.2$ times the object radius listed in the dataset's model information; this single scalar per object is the only
quantity taken from the CAD models (\cref{sec:supp-disclose}).

\paragraph{Energy.}
The field defines an energy of a pose hypothesis given the observation, the foreground-weighted squared token distance
\begin{equation}
E(g;\zobs) = \frac{\sum_p w_p\, \tfrac{1}{D}\|\zhat_p(g)-\zobs_p\|^2}{\sum_p w_p},
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
with $D=384$.

\subsection{Fitting the world model}
\label{sec:wmtrain}
The field is fitted to the cached tokens of the training crops at their ground-truth poses, by minimising $E(g^\star;\zobs)$ of
\cref{eq:energy} averaged over the training set, with batch 32, Adam and a cosine learning-rate schedule (60k steps on SPEED+,
100k on T-LESS). The loss is reconstruction only: there is no contrastive or ranking term, and the field never sees a wrong pose
during fitting. On SPEED+ all token weights are 1; on T-LESS the weights $w_p$ emphasise the visible object. The density, and with it
the object's shape, is never supervised; it is whatever best explains the tokens across all training poses. The field is fitted once
per dataset and is not updated afterwards.

\subsection{Comparing inside every denoising step}
\label{sec:den}
At step $t$, the field renders \cref{eq:wm} at each sample's pose $g_t$ in its observation's crop. The denoiser forms one input token
per image patch from the observation, the rendering, their difference, the weight and the field's latent,
\begin{equation}
x_p=\mathrm{Lin}\big([\,\mathrm{LN}(\zobs_p),\ \mathrm{LN}(\zhat_p),\ \mathrm{LN}(\zhat_p-\zobs_p),\ w_p,\ \mathrm{LN}(\hlat_p)\,]\big)+e_p,
\label{eq:tokens}
\end{equation}
with a learned position embedding $e_p$ and all rendered quantities at $g_t$. The latent $\hlat_p(g_t)$ tells the denoiser which part of
the object, in the field's own coordinates, the hypothesis places under patch $p$. It depends on the hypothesis only, not on the
observation, and it is never decoded into coordinates or depth. A conditioning vector $c=\mathrm{Lin}(\mathrm{SiLU}(\gamma_t(t)+\gamma_g(r_t)
[+\gamma_k(k)]))$ combines Fourier embeddings $\gamma$ of the step and of the Lie-algebra coordinates $r_t$ of $g_t$. On SPEED+ it also
includes the crop camera; on T-LESS, as in LiePose, the crop camera enters only through the rendering. $c$ is prepended as a token and
modulates four adaLN~\cite{peebles2023dit} transformer blocks of width 256. The output at the conditioning token is mapped to the
6-dimensional noise $\epsilon_\theta$. The rendering is treated as a constant: no gradient reaches $\WM$.

The network is trained and sampled with the generative process of \cref{sec:background} (noise-prediction loss, 100-step sampler,
weight averaging). Because the rendering depends on $g_t$, the denoiser can read the error of its own hypothesis from the difference
tokens instead of inferring it from image features alone. The rendering costs one field evaluation per ray sample, sample and step; we
report the cost in \cref{sec:runtime}.

\paragraph{Control without the world model.}
To isolate the comparison, the control is the same network with input tokens $[\mathrm{LN}(\zobs_p),\ w_p]$, trained identically. It
sees the same DINOv2 observation tokens, conditioning and pose embedding, so any difference between \method and the control is due to
the rendered quantities alone. The control is a DINOv2 token denoiser, not LiePose, which uses a trained ResNet-34 encoder.

\paragraph{Trained encoder.}
The comparison is defined in frozen DINOv2 space, because that is the space the field renders. The denoiser can still use a trained
image encoder for its own features. In this variant a frozen DINOv2 copy gives $\zobs$ for the comparison, and a second copy, trained
with a $10\times$ lower learning rate, provides its $16\times16$ tokens; each $2\times2$ cell is concatenated, projected and added to $x_p$.
The control with a trained encoder is built the same way. These rows are marked $^\ddagger$.

\subsection{Selecting among samples}
\label{sec:select}
A diffusion sampler returns one pose per run, and independent runs can land in different modes, \eg flipped configurations of a
near-symmetric object. We draw $N$ samples from independent priors and return $\hat g=\argmin_{i} E(g_i;\zobs)$
(\cref{eq:energy}). Selection uses only the observation and the field. We also report a selection rule that uses no world model,
\emph{consensus}: the sample with the most other samples within $10^\circ$ in rotation. $N=16$ for all benchmarks; the main tables use one
sample unless marked otherwise.

\subsection{Information used at test time}
\label{sec:protocol}
At test time \method uses the image crop, its crop camera $k$ (box and intrinsics), the object identity, which selects the object's
tri-planes as in the BOP task where the target objects are given, and the frozen field. No training image is needed at test time.
Crops are ground-truth boxes on both benchmarks. On T-LESS these are the visible box and visible mask, as in LiePose's
protocol~\cite{hsiao2024liepose}; on SPEED+ the box of the spacecraft keypoints projected with the ground-truth pose. Every method we
train uses the same crops. Rows whose crops come from a detector are marked. Apart from these crops, no ground-truth quantity enters
inference.
