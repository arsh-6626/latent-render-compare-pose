\section{A retrieval-referenced world model}
\label{sec:supp-ret}

This section describes an alternative world model, which predicts the tokens at a pose from the nearest training view instead of
rendering them from a field, and reports every result obtained with it. The denoiser, the control, the training budget and the
evaluation are those of the main paper, except where stated. Everything in this section that says \method refers to \method with this
world model.

\subsection{Model}
\label{sec:supp-ret-model}

\paragraph{Reference bank and retrieval.}
For each object $o$, the \emph{reference bank} holds the cached tokens, poses and crop boxes of the training crops,
$\mathcal{B}_o=\{(\zobs_i,g_i,k_i)\}$. When the object's symmetry group $\mathcal{S}_o$ is known, the bank is expanded by it: each crop
appears once per symmetric equivalent $(R_iS,t_i)$, $S\in\mathcal{S}_o$, subsampled to at most 36 (T-LESS) or 60 (SYMSOL-T) equivalents. Given a
query pose $g=(R,t)$, retrieval returns the nearest entry under the key $[\mathrm{vec}(R_{\text{allo}}),\ \lambda t]$,
$\mathrm{ret}(g)=(\zref,\gref,k^{\mathrm{ref}})$. While training both the world model and the denoiser, entries from the observation's own
source image are excluded, so the model never sees its target as a reference. At test time the bank contains training crops only.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
The world model predicts the tokens of the object at pose $g$, in the crop $k$ of the observation:
\begin{equation}
\zhat(g) = \WM^{\mathrm{ret}}\big(\zref,\ \gref \!\rightarrow\! g,\ k^{\mathrm{ref}},\ k\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times384}.
\label{eq:retwm}
\end{equation}
$\WM^{\mathrm{ret}}$ is a 6-block transformer of width 384 whose blocks are modulated (adaLN~\cite{peebles2023dit}) by Fourier features
of the target pose $g$ and of the relative pose $g\,(\gref)^{-1}$. Its input tokens are a linear projection of $\zref$. The energy is
\cref{eq:energy} with this prediction and the reference retrieved at $g$.

\paragraph{Geometry tokens.}
A rotation of half a degree changes DINOv2 tokens very little, but it moves image points by a computable amount. When the camera
intrinsics and the crop box are available, this displacement is given to the model explicitly (\cref{sec:supp-geo}). We use geometry
tokens on SYMSOL-T and SPEED+; on T-LESS the token cache used for this model does not store the per-crop intrinsics.

\paragraph{Training.}
The world model is trained so that its energy ranks poses, not only so that it reconstructs tokens. For each training crop with pose
$g^\star$ we form a candidate set $\mathcal{C}$ (\cref{sec:supp-wm}). The reference is retrieved at a noisy copy of $g^\star$, which
mimics the poses at which the denoiser will query it. With a soft target that accounts for symmetry,
$q_c \propto \exp\!\big(-d_{\mathcal{S}}(R_c,R^\star)^2/2\sigma_R^2-\|t_c-t^\star\|^2/2\sigma_t^2\big)$, where $d_{\mathcal{S}}$ is the rotation
distance to the nearest symmetric equivalent, the loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{WM}} = \mathrm{CE}\Big(\mathrm{softmax}_c\big(-E(g_c)/\tau\big),\ q\Big) + \lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}\,E(g^\star),
\label{eq:wmloss}
\end{equation}
with a learned temperature $\tau$. On SYMSOL-T we add regression targets from in-plane rotations of training images and from rendered pose
pairs (\cref{sec:supp-wm}).

\paragraph{Denoiser.}
The denoiser's input tokens are $[\zobs_p,\ \zhat_p,\ \zhat_p-\zobs_p,\ w_p]$, and the control sets $\zhat:=0$. The conditioning includes a
linear embedding of the crop box on all benchmarks. Retrieval is a nearest-neighbour query on the CPU, once per sample and step.

\paragraph{Differences from the field.}
The retrieved prediction carries the lighting, background and occlusion of the reference view, and its error at the true pose is
bounded by how far the nearest training view is. The field has neither limitation, but it must represent the whole object from tokens
alone, and it needs no reference bank at test time.

\paragraph{Summary.}
On T-LESS the retrieval world model raises AR over its control by 13.5 and 11.6 points at 100k $\times$ 32 (seeds 42 and 43) and
by 6.7 at the matched budget, and by 10.2 with a trained encoder (44.2 to 54.4 at 100k $\times$ 32). The rendered tokens carry the gain: the explicit difference and the weight add 0.9 and 0.1 points over
$[\zobs,\zhat]$. The comparison contributes at low noise (52.4) and not at high noise (40.7, no better than the control). On SPEED+ the
gains are smaller (0.782 to 0.734 at 100k; 0.717 with geometry tokens), and the 400k model with geometry tokens (0.701) is far behind
LiePose (0.234). With a trained encoder, the comparison lowers the score at the intermediate checkpoint from 0.306 to 0.280, and from
0.204 to 0.168 with consensus selection. On SYMSOL-T it lowers the mean rotation error on every shape. Objects never seen by the
denoiser remain out of reach: AR 6.3 against 3.5 for the control.
