\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
\emph{T-LESS}~\cite{hodan2017tless}: 30 texture-less industrial objects with discrete and continuous symmetries, heavy clutter and
occlusion. We train on the PBR and real training images and test on the BOP19 targets of the real test set. \emph{YCB-V}~\cite{xiang2018posecnn}:
21 household objects in cluttered video frames; PBR and real training images, BOP19 test targets. \TODO{YCB-V: fit the field and
train; until then its columns stay empty.}
\emph{SPEED+}~\cite{park2022speedplus}: a single spacecraft. We train only on the synthetic images and test on the full
hardware-in-the-loop \emph{lightbox} and \emph{sunlamp} sets, without using their images in any form during training.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
On T-LESS and YCB-V we report the BOP average recalls of VSD, MSSD and MSPD and their mean AR~\cite{hodan2020bop}, computed with the
BOP toolkit; VSD uses the toolkit's Python renderer (\cref{sec:supp-protocol}). On SPEED+ we report the mean translation error
$e_t$ (m), the mean rotation error $e_q=2\arccos|\langle q,\hat q\rangle|$ (deg), and the official SPEC2021 score
$S=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum(e_q^{\mathrm{rad}}+e_t/\|t\|)$ with the competition's precision thresholds~\cite{park2022speedplus}
(\cref{sec:supp-protocol}), on the full test sets.

\paragraph{Baselines and training budget.}
The main baseline is LiePose~\cite{hsiao2024liepose}, which we retrain with its public code. The matched budget is LiePose's number of
training images: 400k steps with batch 64. Because every noisy pose of \method queries the world model, we use 64 noisy poses per
image instead of LiePose's 256; the control uses the same 64. Ablations use 100k steps with batch 32 and 16 noisy poses per image
for every row, an eighth of the matched number of training images. Our T-LESS LiePose run uses batch 64 where the paper uses 32, and the control and \method draw
from a fixed set of pre-augmented crops (\cref{sec:supp-disclose}). We evaluate the final checkpoint of every run; no checkpoint is
chosen on test data. Published numbers of other methods are copied from their papers and marked by protocol.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The field has three $128\times128\times32$ tri-planes per object, an MLP of width 128 and 32 samples per ray, and is fitted for 60k
(SPEED+) or 100k (T-LESS) steps. The denoiser has 4 blocks of width 256. Sampling uses LiePose's 100-step sampler. Full details are in
\cref{sec:supp-impl}.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main}

\paragraph{T-LESS and YCB-V.}
\Cref{tab:bop} compares \method with LiePose trained by us under identical crops, data and training budget, with the no-world-model
control, and with published methods. \TODO{describe once the rows are filled: (i) \method vs control (the effect of the comparison),
(ii) \method vs LiePose (ours), (iii) where the difference comes from, using the rotation and translation accuracies of
\cref{tab:supp-tless-rt}, (iv) the effect of selection. Do not compare GT-crop rows with detector rows.}
At 100k $\times$ 32, \method raises AR on T-LESS from 40.5 to 72.4 over the control, with all three recalls higher
(\cref{tab:bop}). The gain is largest in translation, where the recall at 2\,cm rises from 26.8 to 80.3, and the rotation recall at
$5^\circ$ rises from 45.7 to 71.6 (\cref{tab:supp-tless-rt}). With an eighth of the training images, \method is 1.3 AR below LiePose
trained by us at the full budget (73.7); this is one seed, and the matched-budget rows are not yet run. At the same budget the
retrieval world model of \cref{sec:supp-ret} raises AR from 41.2 to 54.7 over its own control (\cref{tab:supp-ret-bop}).

\paragraph{SPEED+.}
\Cref{tab:speedplus} reports the full lightbox and sunlamp sets. At 100k $\times$ 32, \method lowers the score from 0.827 to
0.705 on lightbox and from 0.743 to 0.643 on sunlamp relative to the control, with lower rotation and translation errors on both.
At the matched budget (400k $\times$ 64, frozen DINOv2) the gap to the control is similar: 0.854 to 0.672 on lightbox and 0.793 to 0.664 on sunlamp, and 0.776 to 0.565 and 0.688 to 0.568 with 16-sample consensus. The field models gain little from the four times larger budget, and both are far from LiePose at the full budget (0.234 / 0.289; 0.165 / 0.223 with consensus). With a trained encoder (\cref{sec:den}) and 100k steps of batch 64,
\method reaches 0.412 / 0.480. This run differs from the rows above in both the encoder and the batch, and its control with a trained
encoder has not been evaluated, so the two effects are not separated. \TODO{describe the matched-budget rows, the trained encoder and selection,
and the gap to the oracle as an upper bound on what selection could reach. State explicitly that the SPEC2021 entries used the
unlabeled test-domain images and are not under our protocol.}

\paragraph{What the denoiser reads from the field.}
\Cref{tab:abl-den} varies the denoiser's input tokens with the field fixed. Adding the rendered tokens and their difference to the
observation lowers the SPEED+ score from 0.827 to 0.742 (lightbox) and from 0.743 to 0.687 (sunlamp); adding the field's latent lowers
it further, to 0.705 and 0.643, which is \method. Two further rows test whether the comparison alone suffices.
With only per-token comparison statistics and no observation features, the denoiser is far worse than the control (1.516 / 1.507).
Adding the latent recovers part of this (1.318 / 1.326). Training with the raw channels dropped for half the images, so that the
denoiser must also pose from the statistics, does not help (0.758 / 0.724). The observation's own features are therefore needed: the
field's rendering is not precise enough to replace them, and the denoiser uses it as an additional input.

\Cref{tab:wm-disc} measures the world model on its own, independently of the denoiser: how reliably its energy prefers the true pose
over perturbed ones. On SPEED+ the field prefers the true pose over perturbations of $3$--$10^\circ$ in 84\% of cases and of
$10$--$30^\circ$ in 98\%, and over its $180^\circ$ twins in 98\%. Below $1^\circ$ it is at chance (0.52). \TODO{retrieval on SPEED+ under the same
protocol.} On T-LESS the field is no better than retrieval at fine rotations (0.53 at $1$--$3^\circ$), slightly
better at $3$--$10^\circ$ and in translation, and worse at $10$--$30^\circ$ (0.86 vs 0.92). The pose its energy picks among candidates has a
median error of $3.4^\circ$ but a mean of $14.2^\circ$, against a mean of $7.2^\circ$ for retrieval (\cref{tab:supp-wm-design}): the field makes
more gross errors. We have not established why the field is weaker on T-LESS than on SPEED+; the objects are small, texture-less and
