\begin{abstract}
Diffusion models on SE(3) represent the pose ambiguity of symmetric and occluded objects by sampling, but each denoising step is a
feed-forward function of image features and the noisy pose: the network never checks whether its current hypothesis explains the
image. Render-and-compare refiners perform this check, but they need a textured 3D model and a renderer, and run as a separate stage
after an initial estimate. We propose \method, which carries out the comparison inside every denoising step, in a learned feature
space. Its world model is a feature field of the object, fitted once to the frozen DINOv2 tokens of posed training crops, that renders
the tokens the object would produce at any pose hypothesis in the observation's crop. It needs no CAD texture, reference image or RGB
reconstruction. At every step the denoiser receives the observed tokens, the tokens rendered at its current hypothesis, their
difference, and the field's latent. A control that removes only the rendered quantities, with the same denoiser, data and training
budget, isolates the effect of the world model. With ground-truth crops, the world model raises BOP average recall from 47.3 to 75.6
on T-LESS and from 54.6 to 78.8 on YCB-V, and \method exceeds LiePose trained with the same data and budget (73.7 on T-LESS).
Training the field jointly with the denoiser and its encoder raises T-LESS to 82.2 with an eighth of the training images, and to 84.9 when one of 16 samples is chosen by consensus. With
detector crops and a frozen encoder, the world model raises recall from 31.1 to 53.3 on T-LESS and from 32.8 to 54.8 on YCB-V. On the
SPEED+ spacecraft benchmark it lowers the official score of the control from 0.854 to 0.672 (lightbox).
\end{abstract}
