\begin{abstract}
Diffusion models on SE(3) handle the pose ambiguity of symmetric and occluded objects by sampling, but each denoising step is a
feed-forward function of image features and the noisy pose: the network never checks whether its current hypothesis explains the
image. Render-and-compare refiners do perform this check, but they require a renderer and a textured 3D model, and run as a separate
stage after an initial estimate. We bring the comparison into the diffusion process itself, in a learned feature space. A frozen
world model, trained on DINOv2 patch tokens, predicts the tokens of the object at any pose hypothesis from the nearest training view
and the relative pose between them. At every denoising step and for every sample, the denoiser receives the observed tokens, the
tokens predicted at its current hypothesis, and their difference. The same prediction defines an energy that ranks complete samples
at test time without ground truth. The world model is trained once per dataset with a pose-contrastive objective and is never
updated by the denoiser; LiePose's forward process, loss and sampler are kept unchanged, so the effect of the comparison can be
isolated with a control that removes only the predicted tokens. We evaluate on T-LESS and YCB-V under the BOP protocol, on the
SPEED+ spacecraft benchmark with its official score, and on SYMSOL-T, against LiePose trained with the same budget.
