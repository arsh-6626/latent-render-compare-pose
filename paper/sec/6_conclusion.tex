\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\method relies on ground-truth crops on both benchmarks, as LiePose does on T-LESS; with detected boxes and masks the inputs of the
field and of the denoiser change, and we have not evaluated this setting. The field is fitted per object from posed training crops,
needs the object's identity and the crop camera at test time, and does not apply to unseen objects. On T-LESS it uses one scalar per
object from the dataset's model information, the box size. Its precision is bounded by what frozen, pooled DINOv2 tokens can
distinguish and by how well a field fitted from tokens alone captures each object (\cref{tab:wm-disc}): as a ranker on its own, on T-LESS it
makes more gross errors than a retrieval-referenced world model. Rendering at every step makes sampling slower than LiePose (\cref{tab:runtime}). Finally,
our runs use 64 noisy poses per training image instead of LiePose's 256. \TODO{revise once the results are in; add any failure mode seen

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
We moved render-and-compare into SE(3) diffusion. A feature field of the object, fitted once to frozen DINOv2 tokens, renders at every
denoising step and for every sample the tokens of the object at the current hypothesis, and the denoiser conditions on their comparison
with the observation and on the field's latent. The same rendering gives an energy that ranks samples without ground truth. Because the
diffusion formulation is unchanged and the control differs only by the rendered quantities, the experiments isolate what the comparison
